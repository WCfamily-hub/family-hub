Shepherd's Pie

Serves 3–4 | Bake: 60–75 min

Source: New York Times

Ingredients

  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 1 Tbsp olive oil
  • 1 lb ground lean beef
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • Black pepper
  • 1 cup onion, diced
  • 6 oz baby spinach
  • 1 1/2 Tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1/2 lb sweet potatoes (about 2)
  • 1/2 lb russet potatoes (about 2), or use all russet
  • 4 oz Gruyère, grated (1 cup)

Instructions

  1. Prep the veggies: crush the garlic (4 cloves) and divide into two halves. Chop the onion (1 cup). Peel and slice the sweet and russet potatoes into 1/4 in thick rounds.
  2. Reduce the cream: in a small pot over medium heat, combine the heavy cream (1 cup) and half the crushed garlic. Simmer until reduced to 1/2 cup, about 30 min (or skip this step and use less cream later, about 1/4 cup).
  3. Heat the oil (1 Tbsp) in an ovenproof 10-inch skillet over medium-high heat. Add the beef (1 lb) and brown well, crumbling as it cooks. Season with the salt (1/2 tsp) and a few grinds of pepper, then transfer to a plate.
  4. Add the onion to the pan drippings and cook, stirring occasionally, until tender and golden, about 10 min. Add the remaining garlic and return the meat to the skillet. Stir in the spinach (6 oz), a handful at a time, until wilted. Add the Worcestershire sauce (1 1/2 Tbsp).
  5. Heat the oven to 350°F. Layer half the potato slices over the meat, alternating types and overlapping slightly. Season lightly with salt and pepper and top with half the Gruyère. Repeat with the remaining potatoes and cheese. Spoon the reduced cream evenly over the top.
  6. Cover tightly with foil and bake until the potatoes are very tender, 60–75 min. Uncover and cook until golden brown, about 10 min more. Cool 10 min before serving.

Tip: The potatoes here are scalloped rather than mashed, but still very good. To save time, bake the potato rounds separately on an oiled sheet pan (~25 min) while the meat cooks, then layer the cooked potatoes and cheese over the meat and cover briefly just to melt the cheese before serving.
